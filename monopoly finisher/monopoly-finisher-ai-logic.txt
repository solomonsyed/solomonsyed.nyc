MONOPOLY FINISHER: HOW THE AI PLAYS
=====================================
This documents the actual decision logic in the simulation engine, as
implemented in index.html. Every rule below is quoted from working code,
not a design intention. This is what the AI really does.

There is no lookahead, no minimax, no learning. Every player is governed
by the same fixed set of hand-written heuristic rules, applied identically
to whichever player's turn it is. "Strong AI" here means "plays sensible,
consistent, cash-aware Monopoly," not "provably optimal." Nobody has ever
solved optimal Monopoly play, because full trading between AIs has no
established best strategy. The rules were tuned by running thousands of
simulated games and checking the results felt right (balanced fresh-game
odds, correct favorite in a lopsided example game), not derived from
theory.

------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BUYING AN UNOWNED PROPERTY (wantsToBuy)
------------------------------------------------------------------
When a player lands on a property nobody owns, they decide whether to buy
it outright (at listed price) or let it go to auction.

Rule, in order:
  - If they can't afford the price, they don't buy (forces an auction).
  - Railroads and utilities: buy if doing so leaves at least $40 cash
    in hand afterward. These are treated as always-worth-it if affordable,
    since their rent scales just from owning more of the same type.
  - Streets, if they already own at least one property in that color
    group: buy if it leaves at least $40 in hand. Advancing toward a
    monopoly is treated as worth almost any affordable price.
  - Streets, if they own none of that group yet: buy only if it leaves a
    cash buffer of at least $120, PLUS $6 for every house/hotel currently
    on the board that belongs to an opponent. In other words: the more
    dangerous the board already is (opponents' hotels sitting around),
    the more cash a player insists on keeping in reserve before
    speculating on a brand-new, unrelated color group.

If they decline, the property is auctioned (see #2).

------------------------------------------------------------------
2. AUCTIONS (auction)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Every active (non-bankrupt) player submits one silent bid, computed as:

  - Start from the property's listed price as a baseline value.
  - Streets: if buying it would complete their monopoly (they already
    own every other property in that color group), value jumps to
    2.4x the listed price. If they already own at least one in the
    group (but not the full set), value is 1.3x price. Otherwise it's
    just the listed price.
  - Railroads: value is price times (1 + 0.25 x number of railroads
    they already own). Each railroad you already hold makes the next
    one more attractive.
  - The computed value is then randomized: multiplied by a random
    factor between 0.7 and 1.1, so bidding isn't perfectly predictable
    between otherwise-identical situations.
  - Each player's actual bid is capped at their value estimate OR their
    cash minus a $60 reserve, whichever is lower, so nobody bids their
    whole bankroll.
  - Highest bid wins and pays their bid (not the listed price) to the
    bank. If nobody bids above $0, the property stays unowned.

------------------------------------------------------------------
3. BUILDING HOUSES (build)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Checked once at the end of every turn (after movement/landing resolve),
for every color group the current player fully owns (a monopoly),
in this fixed priority order:

    orange > red > light blue > pink > yellow > green > dark blue > brown

(Orange and red are prioritized because they're statistically the most
landed-on properties in Monopoly, from the Jail-to-Go Chance/Chest/dice
distribution, so they're the best return on investment.)

For each qualifying monopoly:
  - Skipped entirely if any property in that group is mortgaged.
  - A spending target is set based on current cash: build up to 5 houses
    per property if cash > $800, up to 4 if cash > $450, otherwise up
    to 3. ("5" on a single property means a hotel, since hotels replace
    the 5th house slot in this engine's rent table.)
  - Houses are added one at a time to whichever property in the group
    currently has the FEWEST houses (keeping the group as visually and
    strategically "even" as real Monopoly rules require), stopping when:
      - that property has hit its target house count, or
      - building would drop cash below a fixed $180 safety buffer.
  - This repeats across all qualifying groups in priority order, so a
    player might build a little on orange, then move to red, etc., all
    in the same turn, as long as cash allows.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4. JAIL DECISIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------
Checked at the start of a jailed player's turn, after rolling:
  - Doubles: always gets out immediately for free and moves.
  - Otherwise, the player measures "opponentImprovements," the total
    number of houses/hotels currently owned by anyone else on the board.
    This is the AI's proxy for "how dangerous is it out there right now."
  - If that danger score is LOW (fewer than 6 total improvements on the
    board) and they can afford it (cash > $120), they choose to pay the
    $50 bail and get out immediately, preferring to keep playing normally.
  - If the board is dangerous (6+ opponent houses/hotels out there) they
    deliberately STAY in jail and let the turn pass rather than risk
    landing on a loaded property. Sitting still is treated as a valid
    defensive move, not a wasted turn.
  - A player automatically gets out on their 3rd turn in jail regardless
    (standard Monopoly rule: you can't stay in jail forever), and a
    Get-Out-Of-Jail-Free card is used automatically the instant a jailed
    player has one, before any of the above logic runs.

------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PAYING A DEBT: THE RAISE-CASH LADDER (pay / raiseCash)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Whenever a player owes money (rent, tax, a card effect) and doesn't have
enough cash sitting in hand, they try to raise it in this exact order,
stopping as soon as they've covered the debt:

  Step 1: Sell houses back to the bank at half price.
    Repeatedly sells the single house/hotel currently sitting on
    whichever of their own properties has the MOST houses, one unit at a
    time, until either the debt is covered or they're out of houses to
    sell. (Selling from the most-built property first keeps their
    remaining groups more even, mirroring real Monopoly house-selling
    rules.)

  Step 2: Mortgage properties, cheapest first.
    Once no more houses can be sold, they start mortgaging their own
    unmortgaged properties for half their listed price, cheapest
    property first. A property can't be mortgaged while any house is
    still standing anywhere in its color group, so this only kicks in
    once Step 1 has genuinely cleared a group's houses.

  Step 3: Fire sale (only if Steps 1 and 2 together still aren't enough).
    See section 6 below. This is the "distress trading" you specifically
    asked to include.

  Step 4: Bankruptcy.
    If even a fire sale can't raise enough, the player is declared
    bankrupt. Whatever they owed goes unpaid past this point; their
    remaining cash and properties transfer to whoever they owed (or
    back to the bank, if the debt was to the bank itself, e.g. a tax).

------------------------------------------------------------------
6. FIRE SALES: DISTRESS-ONLY TRADING (fireSale)
------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the ONLY trading in the game. There is no proactive dealmaking.
AIs never trade to complete a set unless a player is already this close
to bankruptcy. It exists purely as a last-resort survival mechanism, and
this was a deliberate, discussed design choice (see below on what it
does and doesn't fix).

Trigger: a player has already sold every sellable house AND mortgaged
every eligible property, and is STILL short of the cash they owe.

Process:
  - Their remaining unmortgaged properties are lined up most-valuable
    (highest listed price) first.
  - For each one, every other still-solvent player privately values it:
      - Base value = 35% of the property's listed price (a distressed,
        below-market starting point).
      - +full listed price on top of that if acquiring it would complete
        that bidder's own color-group monopoly (this is the mechanism
        that lets a fire sale hand someone a monopoly, confirmed
        acceptable by you earlier in this project).
      - Railroads get a smaller flat bonus per railroad the bidder
        already owns instead of the monopoly bonus.
      - Each bidder's actual offer is capped at their cash minus a $50
        reserve, so nobody bids themselves into their own bankruptcy to
        rescue someone else.
  - The highest bidder buys it at their bid price, paid directly to the
    distressed seller (not the bank). The seller accepts automatically,
    because a fire sale isn't a negotiation; whatever the best offer is,
    they take it, since the alternative is bankruptcy this turn.
  - This repeats, one property at a time, stopping the moment the seller
    has raised enough cash to cover the original debt (they don't sell
    everything if selling one or two properties was enough).
  - If NO solvent player can afford (or wants) any of the offered
    properties, the fire sale simply raises $0 and the player goes
    bankrupt anyway on the next step. This is treated as a legitimate,
    expected outcome (everyone being cash-poor at once), not a bug.

------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS DOES AND DOESN'T SOLVE (worth knowing)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Because trading is distress-only and never proactive, a full FRESH game
(started from Go, nobody owning anything) stalls out to the simulation's
turn cap roughly 60% of the time, because nobody ever completes enough
monopolies to force the issue, so cash just circulates. From a realistic
mid-game position (the actual intended use case, where you already own some
color groups, some houses are built), stalling essentially never happens,
because the existing monopolies drive the game to a real conclusion.

If a distressed player's board has properties nobody wants or can afford
(e.g., everyone else is also cash-poor), the fire sale can fail entirely
and that player just goes bankrupt with unsold property, which then
passes to whoever they owed, same as if there were no fire-sale system
at all for that particular game.

------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE THIS LIVES IN THE FILE
------------------------------------------------------------------
All of the above is in the <script id="engineCode"> block of index.html,
inside the Game class. Key method names, if you ever want to find or
tweak a specific rule: wantsToBuy(), auction(), build(), turn() (jail
logic is inline near the top), pay(), raiseCash(), fireSale(),
declareBankrupt(). The dollar thresholds mentioned throughout ($120,
$180, $450, $800, $40, $60, $50, the 0.35/2.4/1.3 multipliers, etc.) are
all plain numeric literals in that code. Nothing is configurable through
the UI, so changing AI behavior means editing those numbers directly in
the file.
